Spirography

June 2026

The spirograph toy generates mesmerizing patterns by rolling a small circle inside or around a larger circle while a pen traces the path of a point on the rolling circle. This visualization explores those same mathematical patterns—hypotrochoids and epitrochoids—through an interactive parametric view where you adjust the circle sizes and pen distance to see infinite variations unfold.

About This Visualization

  • Hypotrochoid (rolling inside)
  • Epitrochoid (rolling outside)

Adjust the outer radius, inner radius, and pen distance to generate different spirography patterns. Each combination of parameters creates a unique curve—some simple and symmetric, others dense and intricate.

Parameters: Outer radius sets the fixed circle, inner radius sets the rolling circle, and pen distance determines how far the tracing point is from the rolling circle's center.

Spirography is one of the clearest examples of how simple mechanical rules generate complex, beautiful patterns. A child with a spirograph toy discovers this accidentally—just pick some circle sizes and start drawing. But the mathematics underneath is elegant: parametric equations map the rolling motion to the traced curve, and small changes to the radii or pen distance completely transform the shape.

The pattern closes when the rolling circle has rotated through a rational number of turns—the least common multiple of the two radii determines the period. Some parameter combinations produce tight, almost unrecognizable loops; others fill the entire space with balanced, symmetrical arms. There is something deeply satisfying about watching order emerge from a rule you can understand.
