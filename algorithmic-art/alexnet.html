The fully connected head

AlexNet's classifier is three Linear layers that take the flattened 256×6×6 = 9216 feature vector produced by the convolutional stack and map it through 4096 → 4096 → 1000 neurons to the final ImageNet logits. These three layers alone hold about 58.6 million parameters, or roughly 96% of the entire network.

The scene spans the full AlexNet pipeline: the RGB image input, five convolutional stages with their pooling, the flatten step, and the three fully connected layers that produce the final 1000-class logits. Each stage is drawn as a free-floating cluster of 128 translucent interactive neurons sized from its real tensor shape, so the dramatic geometry of the network — tall early conv feature stacks shrinking spatially while growing in channels, then exploding into a 9216-wide flat vector that's compressed back down to 1000 — reads at a glance, without any bounding boxes or labels getting in the way. Hovering a neuron fires it: the sphere jumps to full opacity, ignites with a hot yellow emissive, and (for FC layers) lights up its weighted connections to the next layer.

Reading neurons and connections

Every stage carries 128 translucent neuron spheres at rest. Hovering one fires it — the node turns opaque, its emissive flips on, and it scales up. For FC layers, firing additionally draws the neuron's connections to the next layer as 3D tubes whose thickness encodes |w| — no numeric labels needed. Conv/image neurons have no per-row weight matrix extracted, so for those firing is the entire highlight. Click to pin / unpin.

How the data was produced

The pretrained weights are loaded with torchvision.models.alexnet(weights=AlexNet_Weights.IMAGENET1K_V1). The script scripts/extract_alexnet.py picks 256 random neuron indices per layer dimension (with a fixed seed), dumps the 256×256 weight submatrices, biases, and per-output-neuron statistics to assets/data/alexnet/fc_sample.json, and the page fetches that JSON at runtime. Rendering uses the vendored Three.js build under assets/js/algorithmic-art/vendor/ with a custom orbit camera controller for drag, wheel zoom, and right-drag pan.
