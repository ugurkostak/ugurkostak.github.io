Nested Polygon Layers

May 2026

Nested Polygon Layers is a beautiful geometric shape composed of nested polygons that gradually decrease in size. Each layer keeps the same basic polygonal rule, but the radius shrinks and the angle shifts, creating a quiet sense of depth as the figure moves inward.

This version treats the form as a parametric design. Sides, layer depth, twist, and shrink define the shells, while straight connections between matching vertices create the illusion of curved petals.

This geometric view organizes repeated polygons into nested layers. Each new layer is smaller and rotated, while the parametric controls change how the structure folds toward the center.

The mathematical idea

The construction starts with a regular polygon. The next layer uses the same number of sides, but its radius is multiplied by a shrink factor and its rotation is advanced by a fixed twist angle. Repeating that rule creates a sequence of similar polygons, each one slightly smaller and slightly turned.

The layered structure comes from the sequence itself: outer shell, middle shells, and inner layer. Connecting each vertex to the corresponding vertex in the next polygon keeps the rule simple, but the accumulated twist makes the straight segments read as flowing curves.

Parametric design

In this sketch, the number of sides sets the polygon template, the layer count controls how many shells are drawn, the twist rotates each shell, and the shrink factor decides how quickly the structure moves toward the center.

Those parameters keep the design readable: every shell follows the same rule, but a small change in twist or layer depth reshapes the whole field. That makes the visualization less like a static diagram and more like a design surface for testing how local rules create global structure.

Why it works visually

Drawing the layers as rings makes the structure readable at a glance. The outer polygon establishes the visible boundary. The middle layers show the rotation and contraction. The innermost layer gives the eye a small center of gravity.

The visualization uses this structure to make a simple polygon rule feel like a layered object. The goal is to let the viewer read the form as outside, shell, inner shell, and center, while still seeing the parametric rule that produced it.
